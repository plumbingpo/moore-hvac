Many Moore, OK homeowners replacing an aging furnace and AC are choosing a single heat pump instead, and the reason is efficiency. A heat pump does not burn fuel or heat a resistance coil; it moves heat. In cooling mode it carries heat out of the house, and in heating mode a reversing valve sends the refrigerant the other way so the compressor gathers heat from outdoor air and releases it indoors. That lets it deliver two to three times more heat than the electricity it consumes. Modern cold-climate equipment with inverter-driven compressors keeps working well below freezing, a genuine engineering leap over earlier generations.

Strong heat pump performance starts with design work, and that is where Moore HVAC earns your trust. A heat pump has to be sized for two loads, summer cooling and winter heating, and the design has to find the balance point where the outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, compare single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter equipment against your climate and ductwork, and plan the backup heat properly, either electric strips staged below the balance point or a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace that takes over in deep cold. We also check your electrical service and ducts, since heat pumps deliver lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need solid airflow to feel comfortable.

On installation day we follow the same standards used on every Moore HVAC system. The line set is pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, the charge is weighed in and checked with superheat and subcooling, and the outdoor unit goes on a stand wherever winter defrost drainage requires it. Condensate is handled properly, and the thermostat is configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost all work as designed. The Benefits of Professional Heat Pump Installation

Since a heat pump runs in both seasons, installation mistakes cost you twice. An incorrect charge cuts cooling efficiency in summer and heating capacity in winter. Skip the balance point calculation and electric backup strips may end up heating the house at several times the operating cost, leaving the homeowner puzzled about why the bills never dropped. Leave the thermostat on factory defaults and it can call for backup heat the compressor could have handled, or interfere with defrost. Set the outdoor unit flat on the ground in a real winter and it sits in refrozen defrost water until the coil turns to ice.

We design and install to prevent every one of those problems. Loads are calculated for heating and cooling, equipment is selected for its actual capacity at your winter design temperature, backup heat is staged and locked out properly, the outdoor unit is raised for defrost drainage, and the charge is weighed and verified instead of guessed. Commissioning covers both modes, including a check of the reversing valve and a full defrost cycle. Refrigerant handling is federally regulated, and inverter equipment brings electronics that reward attention to detail. Installed correctly, a heat pump gives your home the quietest, steadiest comfort available.

Heat Pump Installation Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • A little winter frost is normal; a unit encased in ice is not, so give us a call
  • Rinse the outdoor coil each spring so summer performance starts clean
  • Book twice-yearly maintenance so charge and defrost stay verified in both modes
  • Watch the first winter bill; a sudden spike usually means backup heat is overworking
  • Replace filters on time, since restricted airflow hurts heat pump performance year-round
  • Let the system run steady; big setbacks trigger expensive backup heat on recovery
  • Clear snow, leaves, and plants away from the outdoor unit in every season
