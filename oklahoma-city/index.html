When Your System Fails in Oklahoma City

A few minutes of caution before we arrive can head off a much bigger repair. An iced-over AC should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us right away.

Keeping Your Oklahoma City HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and starting to struggle, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet that same capacitor, ignored for a season, can take out the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off in your home. A quick call saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
